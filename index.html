Stuff

The Tools That Make It Possible

A shared tool library that no single maker could own alone — and one that keeps growing.

It starts with donations. The first tools walk through the door in members' hands — a router here, a drill press there. As the community grows, members vote on what to add next: a 3D printer, a laser engraver, a welder. Every new phase of equipment is a collective decision.

Make

Room to Try, and Try Again

Place plus Stuff: when the right tools live in a space built for making, the whole process changes — you stop planning and start building.

The first version never works. That's not failure — that's iteration. And a makerspace is designed for iteration.

What making looks like here

Prototype in an Afternoon
Walk in with a concept. Walk out with something you can hold. The tools are there; so is the time.
Fail Cheap, Fail Fast
PLA is cheap. Plywood is cheap. Breaking a prototype in the shop is infinitely cheaper than a production run.
Work at Scale
The 4×8 CNC table, the kiln, the full-size table saw — things you simply can't have at home.
Leave It Better
Clean the machine, hang the tool. Making culture is built on mutual respect for the shared space.

Share

Skills Pass from Maker to Maker

Place plus People: when a community gathers in one space, every piece of knowledge becomes available to everyone in the room.

The person next to you just finished a joinery class. The one across the aisle has been soldering for 20 years. You didn't know either of them an hour ago.

How sharing happens

Open Shop Nights
Drop in, work on whatever you're working on, and inevitably answer two questions and ask three more.
Member-Led Classes
The experts in the room teach the beginners. Everyone gets to be both, depending on the day.
Apprenticeship
A mentor and a learner, side by side, working on a real project together. No curriculum. Just doing it.
Show & Tell
Bring what you made. Tell us how. Inspiration spreads faster than any curriculum could teach.

Learn

A Missing Skill Isn't a Dead End

People plus Stuff: hands-on knowledge, right when you need it. The makerspace is a living classroom where the subject is whatever you're making.

She'd never touched a router. By the end of her second visit she was explaining runout to someone who'd just walked in.

How learning happens

Just-in-Time
You learn the technique when the project demands it — not from a syllabus, but from necessity.
Hands On First
Twenty minutes at the machine teaches more than two hours of watching video. Touch things. Break things. Fix them.
Peer Teaching
The person who learned it last month explains it best — they still remember what it felt like not to know.
Certifications
Formal training pathways for the tools that need them: welding, CNC, the laser. Skills that transfer beyond the shop.
